The Stamp Act of 1765 In 1764, the British Parliament passed a new law called the Stamp Act, which imposed a tax on all paper products in return; this affected all colonists, including printers, publishers, lawyers, and customers. Britain created this tax as a result of a very taxing war known as the “French and Indian War.” The problem with this tax was that the citizens of Georgia didn't have a say/vote in any of this, so a group called the "Sons of Liberty" was formed. (George III). The importance of the Stamp Act was in the fact that it caused the first major protest against British rule in the American colonies. The Stamp Act was the first direct tax imposed by Britain, targeted specifically at the colonists. After the “crown” put this “direct tax” on the people, it started an uproar; This tax affected almost everyone as it targeted everyday uses such as newspapers and legal documents. More importantly, it challenged the colonists’ belief, which made them think more about how they should not be taxed without representation in Parliament. Thus, the saying “No taxation without representation” became a popular protest chant. This was a large part of what led to the American Revolution. ("The Stamp Act of 1765"). The Liberty Boys were formed in direct defiance of the Stamp Act that Britain passed, and staged many protests. These protests were originally for the Stamp Act, but with widespread support, they also fought for other things deemed wrong in the eyes of the changing ideals. An example of this is known as the Boston Tea Party. ("Resolutions of the Stamp Act Congress"). The Boston Tea Party happened on December 16, 1773, in direct defiance of the tax Britain put on tea. In which American colonists disguised as Mohawk Indians invaded British ships and dumped 342 chests of tea into the Boston Harbor. The goal of this was to destroy the monopoly of the East India Company and oppose the tax on tea from Britain and because once again they were given no representation on this tax, which angered them. This and the other taxes Britain put on them only fueled their frustration. ("Virginia Stamp Act Resolution") The widespread resistance to the Stamp Act, including protests, boycotts, and the formation of groups like the Sons of Liberty, helped unite the colonies in a common cause. It set the stage for future organized resistance and was a key step on the path toward the American Revolution. ("Resolutions of the Stamp Act Congress").
